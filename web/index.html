05:45 · Accra, Ghana

Less solar wasted. Less diesel burned.

Aegis-Grid turns household batteries into one virtual power plant. They store the solar power wasted at noon, then sell it to the shop next door when load shedding hits, paid with mobile money and settled by a smart contract.

07:00 · Onboarding

A battery owner joins with just a phone number.

A smart meter is fitted between the home's hybrid inverter and its battery. The owner signs in with MTN MoMo: no seed phrase, no gas fees. The battery is registered on-chain as a storage node, and this household becomes a seller.

12:00 · The problem

At noon the sun makes more power than anyone can use.

Community solar peaks while home batteries are small, and there is no market to trade the surplus. So the extra energy is curtailed: clean power that was produced, paid for, then thrown away.

12:30 · Smart surplus charge

Aegis stores the surplus instead of wasting it.

The digital twin spots surplus on the local feeder and signals the seller's meter. The battery charges at the cheapest rate of the day, then stops at 80% so it stays healthy for years.

17:30 · Peak load

Everyone comes home. The grid starts to strain.

Lights, fans and fridges switch on across the city at once. The grid cannot meet the evening peak, so the utility schedules load shedding for this feeder from 19:00.

19:00 · Load shedding

The lights go out, and the generators start.

Down the street, a small shop keeps cold drinks and fish in a freezer. Without power the stock spoils, so the owner starts a diesel generator. Black smoke and noise fill the street where children play.

19:02 · Request clean power

With Aegis, the shop owner taps one button instead.

The buyer requests clean power and leaves a small MoMo deposit. Aegis looks for stored energy on the same transformer, within 500 m, to keep cable losses low. It finds the seller's battery 180 m away, full and ready.

19:03 · Peer-to-peer discharge

A neighbour's battery keeps the shop open.

The seller's hybrid inverter turns stored DC into 230 V AC and sends it down the street cable to the shop. The freezer hums again. The generator stays off, and the battery never drops below 20%.

21:00 · Dual-node verification

Two meters agree on every kilowatt-hour.

The seller's meter counts the energy that left. The buyer's meter counts the energy that arrived. Both sign their readings in hardware and exchange them over LoRa, even with the mobile network down. Nobody can fake a kilowatt-hour.

21:01 · Settlement

The escrow pays everyone in one transaction.

When the two readings agree within 3%, the smart contract on Base pays only for the energy the shop actually received. The seller gets 83%, the utility keeps a 15% grid fee for its cables, and Aegis takes 2%. Any unused deposit goes straight back to the buyer.

23:00 · Proof of impact

A quiet street, clean air, and power that was never wasted.

Every trade is recorded on-chain with the diesel it replaced. Noon sunshine became evening light, the seller earned, the buyer saved, and nobody built a new power station to make it happen.

Who wins

Every party in the trade comes out ahead.

Aegis-Grid turns idle home batteries into shared grid infrastructure. Everyone involved earns or saves something, and nobody has to lose money for it to work.
